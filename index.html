Georgia Tech ME 2110 · Team project

Autonomous Competition Robot

19thout of about 80 teams

Challenge

Build an autonomous robot that could complete several themed tasks within a strict size, cost, and time budget. The robot had to coordinate multiple mechanisms and handle unexpected sensor signals.

My contribution

I wrote the Teensy’s C++ state machine to coordinate motors, pneumatic actuators, solenoids, and limit switches. I designed and assembled a cascading telescoping pole with a motor-driven winch, using SolidWorks and Onshape. I also troubleshot power distribution, MOSFET driver rails, and electrical noise.

Design and testing

I added limit-switch debouncing and logic to handle out-of-sequence inputs. Tests showed that several actions needed to run in a fixed order after one trigger, so I used millis() to time those actions within the state machine. I chose a cascading pole to shorten extension time and simplify the string path.

Outcome

We tested against our specification sheet across three sprints. The build cost $26.37, and the robot averaged about 34 seconds of task time before placing 19th in the final competition.

ARCS · Project manager and electrical team lead

Autonomous Wall-Climbing Robot

5 of 7major cracks detected and reached

Challenge

In three weeks, build a robot that could adhere to a vertical concrete wall, locate cracks, navigate to them, and fill them. Two electric ducted fans provided adhesion, making fan power and battery mass central to the design.

My contribution

I led the electrical team and coordinated subteams through Jira. I designed KiCad power distribution schematics with buck converters, built a current budget for nominal and peak loads, and helped tune the motor PID controllers and integrate ESP32 wireless communication. The robot used Raspberry Pi and YOLO segmentation for crack detection.

Design and testing

I compared LiPo configurations by mass, voltage, capacity, discharge rating, and estimated runtime. We selected 4S 1500 mAh and 6S 1300 mAh packs. Their labeled 120C ratings implied theoretical discharge currents of 180 A and 156 A. We tested the robot on a vertical concrete rig with physical and software emergency stops.

Outcome

The robot detected and navigated to five of seven major cracks. The filling mechanism worked under manual control. Calibration between navigation and the repair mechanism limited fully autonomous filling.

ERAS · Test hardware, avionics, and telemetry

Ionic Wind Aircraft

3 weeks15-student development team

Challenge

Develop a solid-state aircraft powered by ionic wind. The propulsion system’s low thrust imposed a strict mass budget as we worked to integrate thrust vector control and high-voltage electronics.

My contribution

I designed an adjustable launch test stand in Onshape to give the aircraft a controlled initial velocity. I integrated Raspberry Pi and Arduino avionics, used accelerometer and gyroscope data to support thrust vector control, and helped distribute power to the avionics and propulsion electronics. I also built a Python and OpenCV pipeline that tracked an onboard LED to measure position and displacement over time.

Design and testing

We used Arduino Uno boards to simplify integration on a three-week schedule and added a relay-based emergency shutoff for the high-voltage propulsion system. As components were added near the front, the aircraft’s center of gravity shifted forward.

Outcome

The tests validated the launch stand and optical tracking. The aircraft pitched nose-down and did not sustain flight. The flight data pointed to redistributing mass and refining thrust vector control for the next iteration.
